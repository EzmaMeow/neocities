<data>{"title":"'Global' and 'Shared' Objects"}</data>[tab]This is a term I like to use over singletons. I use it for objects that are accessible at or near global level.[tooltip=either by'object','window.object', or 'someNamespaceObject.']\*[/tooltip] Shared objects are a bit difficult to explain and a term I made up when working in Godot.[tooltip=What I called Resource with logic or state that was declared in the file systems and used between many.]*[/tooltip] They can be temporary or permanent objects, but usually given or accessed indirectly unlike global objects.[tooltip=Such as from a parameter or by calling a getter function.]\*[/tooltip] Both are designed to allow different modules or systems to access them and their goals are to share data.

[tab]For me I have a few rules for these objects and that is that they should mostly work independently and they should have a way to clean up references assign to them or dynamic objects they create. This is more to prevent accidental circle dependencies and memory leaks since many objects may gain a reference to them or they will be handling many indirect references.[tooltip=The reference is either assumed to be a generic object or its own adaptor/agent instance. It will not know who is using it nor any unique feature of that agent.]*[/tooltip]

[tab]Global objects tend to be major systems, subsystem, or states and may or may not be singletons. It may also be the main program class, but I tend to rather make a separate global state instead.[tooltip=Another one of my rules: one that knows all should not be directly known by all. So if the main object for the program does not 'know all', then this would not apply.]* [/tooltip] They provide data as long as one knows where they exist and knows something about them.

[tab]Share objects could be many things from event states, controllers[tooltip=this emit events that controls an unknown object or a group of objects such as an ai controller or a player controller]*[/tooltip], states, agents, systems, or anything that needs to be shared among unknown systems (with or without knowledge about one systems). Their main purpose is to be an abstract way provide data or connections between two systems or a system and may objects. How they are received is dependent on how the program is design and their role (which would be too much to explain here).
